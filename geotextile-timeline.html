Pre-Industry Foundations
1861 — Gescher, Germany
Huesker
H. & J. Huesker & Co.
Founded as a textile manufacturer during Germany's industrial expansion. In 1958 pivoted to synthetic textiles, producing tarpaulin filter fabrics and sandbags — the first geotextile-related products. Established US subsidiary in 1991.
Textile roots
1879 — Bologna, Italy
Maccaferri
Officine Maccaferri S.p.A.
Blacksmith shop that reinvented the ancient gabion using wire mesh. Expanded into Canada (1970s) and USA (Williamsport, MD). Today a global leader in hydraulic works, rockfall protection, and geosynthetics with €670M revenue.
Gabion pioneer
2006→ Linear Composites (UK)
2006→ BMD Texteis (Brazil)
2025→ Synteen Technical Fabrics (USA)
1916 — Belton, South Carolina
Belton Industries
Woven geotextile manufacturer
Started as a cotton packaging manufacturer. Entered woven synthetic fabrics in 1979, shifting focus to geotextiles in the 1980s. Beltech® brand woven geotextiles are NTPEP approved for DOT projects across North America.
Woven specialist
1924 — Deadwood, South Dakota
AMCOL / CETCO
Colloid Environmental Technologies Co.
Began as Bentonite Mining & Manufacturing in 1924. CETCO formed in 1991 as subsidiary for environmental products. Acquired Claymax in 1995 becoming a leader in geosynthetic clay liners (GCLs). Now part of Minerals Technologies Inc.
Bentonite/GCL pioneer
1995→ Claymax Corp (GCL technology)
~1940s — United States
Lumite Inc.
Originally a Johnson & Johnson business unit
Founded during WWII as part of the war effort. Became a specialist in monofilament engineered fabrics for filtration, horticulture, and erosion control. Thrace Group purchased 50% in 2006, gaining entry into the US geotextile market.
WWII origins
1704 — Netherlands
TenCate Geosynthetics
Royal Ten Cate / Koninklijke Ten Cate
Dutch textile company with roots in the 18th century. Built a global geosynthetics business spanning nonwovens, wovens, geogrids, and geotextile tubes. 9 production locations worldwide.
Royal heritage
2021→ Acquired by Solmax
1958–1980 — Birth of Geosynthetics
1958 — Cincinnati, Ohio
Carthage Mills
America's FIRST geosynthetics company
Pioneered the first woven synthetic filter cloth used in shoreline protection — replacing costly graded granular filters. Worked with the US Army Corps of Engineers to develop the first Filter Fabric Handbook (1960s). USACE issued the first comprehensive filter fabric specification in 1972, based on Carthage's innovations. Over 3,500 projects by 1976.
★ Industry founder
1972 — Houston, Texas
GSE Environmental
Gundle Lining Systems / Schlegel Lining Technology
Pioneered the first HDPE geomembrane in 1972. Gundle and Schlegel merged to form Gundle Environmental Systems (GSE), going public on NYSE in 1986. Became a global leader in containment systems.
HDPE pioneer
2017→ Acquired by Solmax
1952 / 1984 — Espelkamp, Germany
Naue
Naue GmbH & Co. KG
Started in 1952 as car seat cushion manufacturer. The Terrafix® nonwoven was first used in hydraulic engineering in 1967. Naue Fasertechnik founded 1984. Invented Bentofix® GCL in 1987. Established NAUE America, Inc. in 2008.
Geosynthetics innovator
1970s — United States
Amoco Fabrics & Fibers
(became BP Amoco, then Propex)
Major producer of woven and nonwoven geotextiles. The Amoco geotextile brand was one of the most specified in North America throughout the 1980s and 1990s. Sold to investors forming Propex in 2004.
Legacy brand
2004→ Sold to form Propex
1978 / 1983 — Blackburn, UK / Alpharetta, GA
Tensar
Netlon → Tensar International → Tensar Corporation
Dr. Brian Mercer invented the Tensar® polymer geogrid process in 1978 — a revolutionary method of stretching extruded polymer grids to align molecular chains. Tensar Corporation formed in 1983 as a joint venture. TriAx® hexagonal geogrid launched 2007.
★ Geogrid inventor
2006→ Tensar Corp acquires Tensar International
2014→ Acquired by Castle Harlan ($400M)
2022→ Acquired by Commercial Metals Co (CMC)
1982 — Appleton, Wisconsin
Presto Geosystems
Division of Presto Products Co.
Presto co-invented geocell cellular confinement technology with the US Army Corps of Engineers in the late 1970s. Introduced GEOWEB® system to civil engineering market in early 1980s. Products used on every continent.
★ Geocell inventor
~1970s — Old Hickory, Tennessee
Typar Geosynthetics
Fiberweb / Polymer Group Inc. / Magnera
Typar® nonwoven geotextiles have been manufactured for over 40 years in Tennessee. Originally part of BBA Fiberweb, then spun off as Fiberweb (2006). Acquired by Polymer Group Inc. (PGI). Now part of Magnera Corporation.
Established nonwoven brand

Current Landscape

The Great Consolidation

The North American geotextile industry has undergone dramatic consolidation. Solmax — founded as a small Quebec distributor in 1981 — now owns GSE, TenCate Geosynthetics, and Propex, making it the undisputed global leader. Tensar, the inventor of geogrids, was acquired by Commercial Metals Company. Maccaferri acquired Synteen in 2025, doubling down on the North American market.

Meanwhile, independent manufacturers and distributors — Carthage Mills, Layfield, Hanes Geo, Titan Environmental, Winfab, SKAPS, US Fabrics, Soleno, Strata Systems, Belton, Huesker, Naue, ACE, Thrace/Lumite, Typar — continue to serve the market with specialized products and regional expertise, ensuring healthy competition and innovation.
